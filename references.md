## Composition quality framework
- Hooktheory "Chord and Melody Metrics" — https://www.hooktheory.com/song-metrics/about — the first 5 axes of the now-6-axis quality framework documented in SKILL.md's "Composition quality metrics" section: Chord Complexity, Melodic Complexity, Chord-Melody Tension, Chord Progression Novelty, Chord Bass Melody. Each metric links to a curated list of exemplar songs from their TheoryTab database. The 6th axis (Arrangement Pacing) extends the framework — see arrangement-theory sources below.
- Hooktheory stable/unstable scale degrees — https://www.hooktheory.com/support/musicreference?concept=music-concepts-stable-unstable-scale-degree — the canonical "is this melody note IN the current chord?" reference. Backs the chord-melody tension axis.
- Hooktheory TheoryTab — https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab — searchable database of chord+melody breakdowns for thousands of popular songs. Browse the Bass Melody / Chord Complexity exemplar lists for new moves worth absorbing.

## Arrangement theory *(sources for the 6th axis: Arrangement Pacing, and the Intra-section arrangement variation catalog)*

The named intra-section arrangement moves in SKILL.md are distilled from these sources. They establish the "Rule of 8" pop-production principle (something must change every 4–8 bars) and provide vocabulary for entry/exit timing, negative space, density curves, and texture interaction.

- EDMprod "How to Avoid the 8-Bar Loop Trap" — https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-avoid-the-8-bar-loop-trap/ — source for subtractive arrangement (macro phase = remove tracks across sections, micro phase = fade in/out within sections), "same idea different instruments" re-orchestration, mid-section drops.
- Sample Focus "Using Space and Silence in Music Production" — https://blog.samplefocus.com/blog/using-space-and-silence-in-music-production/ — source for complete silence vs. near-silence-with-decay distinction, drop-into-silence mechanic, half-time-stop technique, silence-as-transition (alternative to FX sweep).
- Neat Little Studios "Ultimate Guide to Arranging Songs for Better Impact" — https://neatlittlestudios.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-arranging-songs-for-better-impact — source for fake drops, beat switches, single-bar silence reset.
- iZotope "How These 5 Artists Use Negative Space" — https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/how-these-5-artists-use-negative-space-in-music — Prince's minimalist approach, trap/hip-hop sparse arrangements, dubstep frequency-space drops.
- Sound on Sound "Inside Track: Mix Secrets" series — https://www.soundonsound.com/article-name/inside-track-mix-secrets — written analyses of how producers built specific arrangements (long-form, valuable for deep-dive mining if a specific song/genre needs more vocabulary).
- Adam Neely "Elemental Arranging" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UKaonmghNfg — concept of taking atomized musical elements and synthesizing them. Source for the "elemental arranging" framing (named but not extensively quoted in SKILL.md — full transcription would yield more if needed).
- Rick Beato "What Makes This Song Great" YouTube series — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Beato — Beato dissects specific songs' arrangement entry/exit decisions using the stems. Not transcribed but flagged as the densest single source for verified-from-real-songs arrangement vocabulary; consider per-episode mining if specific genres need expansion.
- Samuel Adler "The Study of Orchestration" — classical orchestration textbook. Source for technical terms like *wedge entry*, *stratification*, *Klangfarbenmelodie* (timbre-melody — distributing a melodic line across multiple instruments). Heavy reading; the SKILL.md catalog adopts the terminology, not the textbook structure.
- Morgan Page "The Rule of Eight" — https://mpquicktips.com/2018/06/10/the-rule-of-eight-songwriting-and-the-story-circle/ — songwriter's-angle confirmation of the Rule-of-8 principle as universal across pop forms.
